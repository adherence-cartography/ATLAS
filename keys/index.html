What your Letter of Permission actually covers

Your Letter of Permission records your subscription activation date. It covers data collected during your active subscription period only — it does not reach backward. If you subscribe today to cover a study you already ran, the mismatch between your activation date and your data-collection dates will be visible to any journal editor or IRB officer who verifies your key at keys.adherence.cc/verify. Subscribe at the start of your study, not at the end of it.

Frequently asked questions
What is a workspace key?
A workspace key is a unique alphanumeric code that activates your research environment on the ATLAS platform. It isolates your patient cohort from the global pool, deploys the MAP instrument for your study, enables full PEACS scoring and data export, and includes your Letter of Permission for publication. Your key follows the format TIER-INSTITUTION-SEQUENCE-YEAR.
What adherence instrument is included with a workspace key?
Every paid workspace key includes full access to the MAP instrument — Multidimensional Adherence Parameters — deployed through ATLAS. MAP is an independently developed, psychometrically precise adherence assessment designed for institutional and research use, with 8 independently authored items, Grade 6 reading level, and 60+ language support. A Letter of Permission covering both data collection and publication is issued automatically with your subscription and is verifiable at keys.adherence.cc.
How does sign-in work?
ATLAS uses a two-step sign-in for all workspace keys. Enter your workspace key on the ATLAS entry screen, then check your registered email for a 6-digit one-time code. Enter the code to complete sign-in. The code expires in 10 minutes. No passwords, no app to install. This applies to all keyed roles — Student, Pharmacist, Researcher, PI, Institution, and Observer.
Which tier is right for me?
Student ($19/mo) is for thesis and coursework projects — full MAP instrument, bulk upload, APE phenotyping, PEACS snapshot, and up to 100 record CSV exports per month. Clinician ($49/mo) is for practicing clinicians (PharmD, NP, PA, RN, MD, Care Coordinator) who need MTM encounter logging (CPT 99605–99607), ZOE SOAP AI notes, Sentinel alerts, and the full TPE · PE Domain Analysis suite. PI · Multi-Site ($149/mo) is a research-only workspace for PIs running IRB-approved protocols — adds Validation Study Tools, self-service sub-workspace management, cross-site benchmarking, IRB Submission Assistant, dataset lock, IRB-grade export headers, CONSORT participant flow, and protocol amendment log. Institution tiers are for organisations deploying MAP at scale: Academic ($399/mo) replaces REDCap, SPSS, R, and Excel for university research programs — includes Cross-Study Research Hub, Grant Reporting Dashboard, Student Thesis Module, and native psychometric panel. Health System ($599/mo) replaces Epic MTM modules and Excel-based care gap management for hospitals and health networks — includes Care Gap Monitor (MMAS-8 triggered alerts), MTM Billing Log (CPT 99605–99607), and Population Health Command Center. Academic Medical Center ($799/mo) combines both for research hospitals and AMCs with clinical and academic missions.

Observer seats give board members, funders, and study sponsors full read-only visibility into the platform — global analytics, cohort dashboards, and live data — without write access or the ability to enter assessments. No. Seats issued under an Institution account are covered by the institutional subscription — researchers and students do not pay separately. The institution manages and pays for all seats centrally. What happens to my data if I cancel?
Your data remains in the ATLAS system for 90 days after cancellation. You can export a full CSV of your cohort at any time during this period. After 90 days inactive data is archived. Reactivating your subscription restores full access.
I am at an LMIC university. Do I need to pay?
Not individually. ATLAS has a partnership program for universities in low and lower-middle income countries. Your institution can apply for subsidized or free access. Email info@adherence.cc with your institution name and country.
What is the difference between a Letter of Permission and a Publication License?
A Letter of Permission (LOP) is issued automatically to all paid workspace subscribers as part of their subscription. It covers both ongoing data collection and publication rights, and remains valid while the subscription is active. It expires if the subscription lapses. A Publication License is a separate product for studies that are already complete and had no prior subscription. For students and self-funded researchers, it is a one-time flat rate ($49–$249) issued instantly. For funded researchers and PIs, publication rights are priced by study duration — not a flat fee — because a grant-funded study is not equivalent to a thesis project. Funded researchers should email info@adherence.cc for a custom quote. Both documents are accepted by journal editors, IRB officers, and ethics committees, and both are verifiable at keys.adherence.cc. If you have an active workspace subscription, you already have your Letter of Permission and do not need a Publication License.
Can I subscribe for one month to get a Letter of Permission for data I already collected?
No. A workspace subscription is not a retroactive publication permit. Your Letter of Permission records your subscription activation date — the date you first subscribed — and covers only data collected while your subscription was active. If your study ran for twelve months and you subscribed on the final day, your LOP reflects one day of covered activity. Journal editors and IRB officers who verify your key at keys.adherence.cc/verify see your activation date, your subscription history, and your data-collection dates. The mismatch is immediately apparent. If your study is already complete, the correct path is the Student Publication License (for thesis and self-funded work) or the duration-based funded study inquiry (for grant or institutionally funded research) — not a subscription opened after the fact.
I already collected my data without a subscription. How do I get a letter for my journal?
It depends on how your study was funded. If you are a student or self-funded researcher with a completed thesis or dissertation, the standalone Publication License is the right product — a flat one-time payment ($49–$249 by participant count), uploaded at atlas.adherence.cc?publish=1, with your PUB key and Letter of Permission issued instantly. If your study was grant-funded, institutionally funded, or contract-funded, publication rights are priced by study duration rather than a flat rate. Email info@adherence.cc with your study title, funding source, IRB number, and data-collection dates. A workspace subscription covers both data collection and publication rights — if you collected data without subscribing, the publication fee reflects the duration of that coverage.
How does a journal editor verify a Publication License?
Enter the PUB-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX key in the verification form at the bottom of this page (or at keys.adherence.cc/verify). The registry will confirm the principal investigator name, institution, study title, validated participant count, countries, and issue date. Publication licenses are permanent — they remain active in the registry indefinitely and the verification URL can be shared directly with the editorial office.
What is the difference between MAP and PEACS?
MAP (Multidimensional Adherence Parameters) is an 8-item cross-sectional adherence instrument — independently authored, psychometrically designed for institutional and research deployment, and optimized for accuracy, literacy accessibility, and translational portability across 60+ languages. MAP captures adherence at a point in time and is the recommended instrument when a validated, publication-ready measure is needed. It is the instrument deployed through ATLAS at every workspace tier. PEACS (Predictive Emergence Assessment for Clinical Services) is a 22-item longitudinal instrument that tracks adherence trajectories over time across three subscales: BASE (7 items, monthly), MVMT (7 items, weekly), and STRATA (8 items, quarterly). PEACS calculates the Theory of Predictive Emergence score — PE = (Architecture × Execution × Context)^(1/3) — and is the primary engine for Clinician, Researcher, PI, and Institution tiers. MAP feeds into the PEACS Execution domain, making the two instruments complementary: MAP delivers a precise cross-sectional adherence signal; PEACS builds a longitudinal predictive profile over time.
Can patients access their own results?
Yes, with a Clinician / Researcher tier key or higher. The Patient Portal is accessible via a ?portal=<patient_id> URL and shows the patient their own score history, PE domain profile, personalised adherence tips, and care team contact information. The portal link is included automatically when a coordinator copies the reminder link from the follow-up card, so no manual URL construction is needed. Patients never see other patients' data — access is scoped to a single patient record by the portal token.
Does ATLAS support IRB submission preparation?
Yes, for PI tier subscribers. The IRB Submission Assistant is a 5-tab modal inside the Clinical Study Module (Study tab) that auto-populates from your Study Config. It generates a structured document covering: Protocol Summary, Informed Consent language, Risks & Benefits, Privacy & Data handling, and Timeline & Sites. The document exports as a .txt file ready for attachment to your IRB application. Study Config captures Study Name, Principal Investigator, Institution, Sponsor, IRB Number, ClinicalTrials.gov ID, Protocol Version, Start Date, Data Lock Date, Target N, Follow-up Window, and Visit Schedule — all of which flow through to the IRB export and to IRB-grade export headers on your data files.
