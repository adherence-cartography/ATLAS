# ATLAS Platform — Audit Log Retention Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines retention periods, storage tiers, access controls, and disaster recovery requirements for all audit and operational logs generated by the ATLAS platform (atlas.adherence.cc). It fulfills obligations under 21 CFR Part 11 §11.10(d)(e), HIPAA §164.312(b), and applicable data protection regulations.

---

## 2. Log Categories and Retention Tiers

### Tier 1 — CFR-11 Compliance Records (Permanent Retention)

| Log Type | Storage System | Retention Period | Immutability Mechanism |
|----------|---------------|-----------------|----------------------|
| CFR-11 audit trail (`cfr11: true` entries in `audit_log`) | Firebase Realtime Database + DynamoDB (`atlas_audit_log`) | **Permanent — no expiry** | DynamoDB IAM Deny (DeleteItem / UpdateItem blocked for all roles including Lambda execution role) |
| Electronic signature records (`esignatures/` Firebase path) | Firebase Realtime Database | **Permanent — no expiry** | Firebase Security Rules: write-once, no delete permitted for `esignatures` path |
| SHA-256 payload hashes (embedded in audit entries) | Firebase + DynamoDB | **Permanent** | Co-stored with audit entry; hash verifiable at any time |
| Record deletion audit entries (action: "DELETE") | Firebase + DynamoDB | **Permanent** | Same as CFR-11 trail above |

**Regulatory basis:** 21 CFR Part 11 §11.10(d)(e) requires that audit trail records be retained for as long as required by the applicable regulations — for FDA-regulated studies, records must be retained for at least 2 years after approval or the study end date, whichever is later. ATLAS applies permanent retention as the most conservative and operationally simplest policy.

**Immutability verification:** At any time, the integrity of a CFR-11 audit entry can be verified by recomputing the SHA-256 hash of the stored payload JSON and comparing it to the `payload_hash` field. A mismatch indicates tampering.

---

### Tier 2 — HIPAA Audit Records (6-Year Minimum Retention)

| Log Type | Storage System | Retention Period | Notes |
|----------|---------------|-----------------|-------|
| Workspace access logs (login, session start/end) | Firebase `audit_log` + DynamoDB | **6 years minimum** | Required by HIPAA §164.530(j) |
| Failed login attempts | Firebase `audit_log` | **6 years minimum** | action: "LOGIN_FAILURE" entries |
| Session timeout events | Firebase `audit_log` | **6 years minimum** | action: "SESSION_TIMEOUT" entries |
| Workspace key provisioning/revocation | AWS SSM Parameter Store history | **6 years minimum** | SSM parameter history retained in AWS |
| BAA and DPA execution records | Off-platform document store (Google Drive / DocuSign) | **6 years minimum** | 45 CFR §164.530(j) |
| Privacy Officer designation records | Off-platform (see privacy-officer-memo.md) | **6 years minimum** | From date last in effect |
| Workforce training completion records | Off-platform | **6 years minimum** | 45 CFR §164.530(j) |

**Regulatory basis:** 45 CFR §164.530(j) requires HIPAA-covered entities and business associates to retain required documentation for 6 years from the date of its creation or the date when it was last in effect, whichever is later.

**Enforcement mechanism:** DynamoDB TTL is not set on any table storing HIPAA-relevant records. Firebase data is not subject to auto-expiry rules for the `audit_log` path. Manual deletion of records in these categories requires explicit superadmin action and generates its own CFR-11 audit entry.

---

### Tier 3 — Operational and Diagnostic Logs (90-Day Rolling Retention)

| Log Type | Storage System | Retention Period | Enforcement |
|----------|---------------|-----------------|-------------|
| AWS CloudTrail API call logs | S3 bucket: `atlas-cloudtrail-logs` | 90 days (S3 lifecycle rule) | S3 Lifecycle Policy: transition to S3 Glacier after 30 days; delete after 90 days |
| Lambda function invocation logs | AWS CloudWatch Logs | 90 days | CloudWatch Log Group retention setting: 90 days |
| Cloudflare access and security logs | Cloudflare dashboard (Workers Logs) | 7 days (Cloudflare default) | Cloudflare platform policy; not configurable at this tier |
| Firebase function execution logs | Google Cloud Logging | 30 days | GCP default retention; configurable if needed |
| Application error logs (console/runtime) | AWS CloudWatch Logs | 90 days | CloudWatch Log Group retention setting |

**Note on CloudTrail:** Although operational logs are retained for 90 days, CloudTrail serves a dual purpose — it also satisfies the infrastructure-level audit requirement of 21 CFR Part 11 §11.10(d). If CloudTrail logs are needed for a regulatory investigation or legal hold, they must be exported to a separate long-term archive before the 90-day TTL expires. The Privacy Officer is responsible for initiating such an export upon learning of any regulatory inquiry.

## 3. Access Controls by Tier

| Tier | Who Can Read | Who Can Delete | Approval Required |
|------|-------------|---------------|------------------|
| Tier 1 (CFR-11, Permanent) | Superadmin via Audit Log Viewer; Privacy Officer via AWS Console | **No one** — IAM Deny policy blocks DeleteItem/UpdateItem | N/A — deletion not possible |
| Tier 2 (HIPAA, 6-year) | Superadmin; Privacy Officer; AWS Console root (with MFA) | AWS Console root only, with documented justification | Privacy Officer written approval; retain justification for 6 years |
| Tier 3 (Operational, 90-day) | DevOps / technical staff via AWS CloudWatch / CloudTrail console | Automatic via lifecycle policy | No manual approval required — lifecycle rules govern deletion |

**Superadmin Audit Log Viewer:** The ATLAS Mission Control "Audit Log" tab provides a filtered, read-only view of Tier 1 and Tier 2 records stored in Firebase. It is accessible only to users with `role: "superadmin"` in AWS SSM Parameter Store. All access to the Audit Log Viewer is itself logged.

## 4. Backup and Disaster Recovery

### Tier 1 — CFR-11 Records

- **DynamoDB Point-in-Time Recovery (PITR):** Must be enabled on the `atlas_audit_log` table. PITR provides continuous backups with restore capability to any point in the last 35 days. See `docs/compliance/runbooks/aws-dynamodb-iam-deny.md` for setup instructions.
- **Firebase Backup:** Firebase Realtime Database daily exports should be enabled and stored in a separate GCS bucket with a 90-day retention policy. Exports are JSON-serialized and can be used to reconstruct the audit trail.
- **Cross-region redundancy:** DynamoDB global tables may be configured to replicate the `atlas_audit_log` table to a second region for disaster recovery. This is not enabled by default but is recommended for production deployments under clinical research contracts.

### Tier 2 — HIPAA Records

- **DynamoDB PITR** covers all DynamoDB-stored HIPAA records (same configuration as Tier 1).
- **Firebase export** covers Firebase-stored session and access logs.
- **Off-platform records** (BAA documents, training records) must be backed up according to the organization's document management policy. Minimum: two copies in geographically separate locations (e.g., Google Drive + local encrypted backup).

### Tier 3 — Operational Logs

- **S3 versioning:** The `atlas-cloudtrail-logs` S3 bucket should have versioning enabled to prevent accidental deletion of logs within their retention window.
- **CloudWatch:** Operational logs in CloudWatch are replicated within the AWS region and do not require additional backup configuration for 90-day retention.

### Recovery Time Objectives

| Tier | RTO (Recovery Time Objective) | RPO (Recovery Point Objective) |
|------|------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Tier 1 (CFR-11) | 4 hours (DynamoDB PITR restore) | 0 (continuous PITR) |
| Tier 2 (HIPAA) | 4 hours (DynamoDB PITR restore) | 0 (continuous PITR) |
| Tier 3 (Operational) | 24 hours | 24 hours (CloudWatch SLA) |

## 5. Legal Hold Procedure

In the event of litigation, regulatory investigation, or audit, the Privacy Officer shall:

1. Immediately notify technical staff to suspend any applicable lifecycle rules that would result in deletion of relevant records.
2. Export affected log sets from CloudTrail, CloudWatch, Firebase, and DynamoDB to a secured, non-expiring archive in S3.
3. Document the hold scope, date, and responsible personnel.
4. Maintain the hold until written authorization is received from legal counsel to release it.
